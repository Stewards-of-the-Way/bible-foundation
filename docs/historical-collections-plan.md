# Historical collections (planned, not populated)

- **How We Received the Bible:** dated manuscripts, discovery, textual transmission, early translations, printing, and modern editions; distinguish event-date from discovery-date, date estimates, and scholarly disputes.
- **Church councils and canon history:** distinguish ecumenical doctrinal councils, regional canonical lists, ecclesiastical reception, and differing Christian canons. Attach primary evidence, references, uncertainty and interpretation flags.
- **Divine names and titles:** Hebrew/Greek forms, scriptural occurrences, meaning-in-context, source lexicon, aliases and grammar. Keep distinct from historical human people records, place records, and archaeological locations. No artificial goal of 200 'El names'.
- **Historical witness vs theological inference:** explicitly separate what artifacts establish from faith claims about providential preservation; acknowledge manuscript variants.
- **Translation biographies:** separately researched About pages linking to shared events only when evidence of use/availability supports that connection.
- **Shared history principle:** record shared event once, reference it by stable ID in multiple tools.
